# Failures in the corrected collection

These reviews concern the three-attempt SQL campaign using Syncular 0.17.0, started on 2026-09-08 at 22:58 UTC. They remain attached to specific attempts. [Current coverage](../../COVERAGE.md) includes successful, unavailable and failed outcomes; the examples below are not product failure rates.

PowerSync's setup failures came from accumulated benchmark history in the self-hosted service. Fixture resets removed source rows but we had never run bucket compaction. A September 9 follow-up reproduced the initial-writer readiness failure with 2,000 source tasks and **5,980,513 global bucket operations**. Fresh clients connected both directly and through the benchmark relay still had zero visible rows after 35 seconds. After native compaction reduced that bucket to 2,002 current PUTs and one CLEAR, both fresh clients completed initial sync in about **755 ms** and passed the full 2,000-row validator. This is a diagnostic before/after check, not a replacement benchmark median.

The fix waits for fixture replication and runs native compaction before opening clients, outside measured latency. Persistent volumes remain in place. The one-time repair used the pinned service's compactor with a larger deletion batch; ordinary seeds use its standard command. Indexes, readiness deadlines and correctness checks were not relaxed. The [diagnostic record and artifact hashes](../../results/diagnostics/powersync-bucket-history/EXPERIMENT.json), [original failure](../../results/diagnostics/powersync-bucket-history/reproduction-before.json), [passing replay test](../../results/diagnostics/powersync-bucket-history/reproduction-after.json), and [maintenance script](../../results/diagnostics/powersync-bucket-history/compact-large-batches.mjs) preserve the evidence. The original probe mislabeled its abort deadline as `firstSync`; its zero rows and `hasSynced: false` show that synchronization had not completed.

The original campaign's failed attempts remain available in the [individual setup reviews](../../results/diagnostics/tuned-v017-publication-failures/campaign-2026-09-08T22-58-56-419Z/CASE-REVIEWS.json). They measured no offline recovery, conflict or client-scaling latency. All three bootstrap attempts stopped at 1,000 tasks, reporting zero rows despite roughly 179–196 MB of response traffic; they never reached the displayed 100,000-task case. These were harness preparation failures, not evidence of a PowerSync scaling limit.

All three original Syncular Rust attachment attempts timed out during reader preparation before any upload variant began. A WebSocket-readiness race is a supported hypothesis: the original benchmark server discarded messages until its async session setup finishes, while native connect can return before the server's `hello`. None captured a frame trace proving that this happened. The [final attempt review](../../results/diagnostics/tuned-v017-publication-failures/campaign-2026-09-08T22-58-56-419Z/0118/INSPECTION.json) records the same preparation timeout before any attachment variant. The [inspection and copied source](../../results/diagnostics/tuned-v017-publication-failures/campaign-2026-09-08T22-58-56-419Z/0030/INSPECTION.json) explain the evidence and the proposed buffering experiment. This outcome supplies no attachment transfer measurement. A later [development check](../../results/diagnostics/coverage-fixes/VERIFICATION.json) passes the entire attachment contract after buffering early frames, preserving their order and closing late sessions safely. The regression tests establish the server bug; the rerun alone does not establish the exact cause of the three historical timeouts. A [single-run publication](../../results/reports/coverage-fixes/RESULTS.md) now supplies validated attachment measurements; the old timeouts remain superseded evidence.

Each inspection sits beside its raw result, capture receipt and service logs. Further diagnostics run after fixed collection and stay separate from the comparison samples. [Earlier failure reviews](../history/README.md) belong to the withdrawn campaign.

Turso’s connected-fanout trial 1 and reconnect-storm trial 2 completed their five-reader subcases, then failed during 25-reader setup with `pwrite: no storage space`. These are environment failures; the exact peak footprint was not sampled. Both attempts count toward the three-attempt cap. The [maintenance record](../../results/diagnostics/publication-index-review/MAINTENANCE-PAUSE.json) documents a pause between attempts, lossless archives of idle stores and old working copies, and removal of unused compilation intermediates. The same campaign resumed without changing measured source.

Turso’s second replica-reopen attempt failed earlier, during initial sync, when fetch reported an unexpectedly closed socket. Its [setup review](../../results/diagnostics/tuned-v017-publication-failures/campaign-2026-09-08T22-58-56-419Z/0061/INSPECTION.json) records an open gate and matching server broken-pipe logs. Those observations do not establish which side closed the connection or why. This remains unexplained and supplies no reopen latency; it is not attributed to the separate disk failures.

## Timeout review

The [per-attempt review](../../results/diagnostics/final-publication/TIMEOUT-REVIEW.json) covers **all 14 timed-out attempts currently selected for the README**: five Jazz startup, five Jazz update/delete and four Jazz access-revocation attempts. The three original Rust setup timeouts are superseded by the repaired case. It checks the archived results, logs, deadline source and final snapshots. Raw statuses and timings remain unchanged. Run `python3 scripts/audit-timeouts.py` to verify the evidence again.

- **Jazz startup:** all five hit the actual 90,000 ms deadline during **cold** startup at 100,000 tasks. The final recorded counts were 79,360, 69,120, 85,165, 87,185 and 65,792 rows; neither screen correctness nor full materialization was established. The 1,000- and 10,000-task cold/warm cases had passed. The displayed **warm 100,000-task case never ran**, so its label is now **Not reached**. These are valid cold-startup deadlines, but the transport, query-observation overhead and retained-storage contributions remain unmeasured.
- **Jazz update/delete:** all five reached conflict resolution and hit the 90,000 ms reader-observation deadline. The writer still had 2,000 rows, including its edited target; the peer and observer had 1,999 rows with that task absent. Native write receipts succeeded and queues were empty. This is confirmed disagreement between client views, now labeled **Did not converge**. It does not establish the underlying product or adapter cause, nor validate the assumed native deletion policy.
- **Jazz access revocation:** all eight subcases across four attempts exceeded the 60,000 ms purge deadline. Online queries and subscriptions showed the correct 500 authorized rows, but raw local storage still contained 1,000. After reconnect, all three views still contained 1,000. Independent fresh clients saw the correct authorized data in every attempt. **Purge timed out** describes the failed contract; saying simply that authorization failed would be misleading. Whether the tested SDK promises this purge behavior remains separate from observing that it did not occur.
- **Syncular Rust attachments:** all three returned the native 30,000 ms realtime-sync timeout from reader setup. No attachment variant or initial-state validation began. They remain archived as setup timeouts, with the possible harness readiness race explicit; the README uses the repaired implementation’s new measurement. The record supports a setup deadline error, not an attachment-transfer timeout or a confirmed product defect.

The Jazz trial logs are empty; the useful evidence is embedded in the raw results. Startup and purge include elapsed-deadline receipts. Conflict and Rust setup retain the error and relevant state, but lack a per-wait timing receipt and transport trace. No new benchmark rounds were run for this review. PowerSync’s superseded setup timeouts are covered by the confirmed fixture-history investigation above.
